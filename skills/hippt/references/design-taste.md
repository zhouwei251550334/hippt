# Presentation design taste

Use these rules contextually. Read the audience, delivery setting, content, trust requirements, brand, and selected template before applying any aesthetic preference.

## Contents

- Visual read and three deck dials
- System locks
- Anti-template discipline
- Layout and pacing
- Migration and redesign protocol
- Accessibility and release review

## Visual read

Before slide composition, write one internal sentence:

> Reading this as: [deck type] for [audience] in [setting], using a [tone and trust] visual language, based on [selected template or brand system].

Do not turn this into a long user questionnaire. Infer it from the approved brief and ask only when two directions would materially change the result.

## Three deck dials

Record three values from 1 to 10. They are production constraints, not user-facing scores.

- `LAYOUT_VARIANCE`: 1 is highly regular and symmetrical; 10 is experimental and asymmetric.
- `VISUAL_DENSITY`: 1 is spacious and image-led; 10 is compact and information-heavy.
- `VISUAL_ENERGY`: 1 is quiet and restrained; 10 uses stronger scale contrast, image coverage, color tension, and pacing. High energy does not mean oversaturation or decorative clutter.

Use these starting ranges, then adjust for the template and brief:

| Scenario | Layout variance | Visual density | Visual energy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Medical or scientific | 3-4 | 4-6 | 3-4 |
| Lecture or competition | 5-7 | 3-5 | 5-7 |
| Presales or product | 5-7 | 4-6 | 5-7 |
| Internal report | 3-5 | 5-7 | 3-5 |
| Faithful content migration | Selected HiPPT template baseline | Match content need | Match delivery setting |

Regulated, accessibility-critical, and trust-first settings override aesthetic ambition. Template fidelity never overrides legibility or evidence clarity.

## System locks

Define and keep these stable across the deck:

- brand lock: approved logo, safe area, brand voice, and non-negotiable brand assets;
- palette lock: one coherent neutral and surface family with explicit semantic color roles;
- accent lock: one dominant accent or a documented accent hierarchy, not a new highlight color on each slide;
- shape lock: a consistent radius, border, line, and container system;
- typography lock: the approved role map and weight hierarchy from `typography.md`;
- icon and diagram lock: one icon family, line weight, arrow language, and diagram grammar;
- image-treatment lock: consistent crop, corner treatment, tonal grade, caption, and source-label behavior.

Section dividers may deliberately invert or intensify the system, but random theme changes are not allowed.

## Anti-template discipline

Avoid these defaults unless the content genuinely requires them:

- three equal cards for every topic;
- a rounded box around every text group;
- decorative pills, status dots, version stamps, fake controls, or numbered eyebrows;
- random purple-blue gradients, neon glows, or a different accent on every slide;
- oversized titles used in place of real hierarchy;
- fake dashboards, fake product screenshots, fake customer logos, fake testimonials, or invented metrics;
- stock photography presented as clinical, product, customer, or outcome evidence;
- mixed icon families, arbitrary corner radii, inconsistent shadows, and decorative arrows that encode no relationship;
- identical title-plus-cards compositions repeated throughout the deck.

These are contextual warnings, not absolute bans. Three equal cards are correct when three items are genuinely equivalent. Numbering is correct when sequence matters. Status markers are correct when they represent real state.

## Layout and pacing

- Give every slide one visual job: statement, image, chart, comparison, process, evidence, case, decision, summary, or transition.
- Match the layout family to that job before considering decoration.
- Preserve recurring title zones, margins, palette, and signature template elements while varying the body silhouette.
- Prefer a distinct source layout or derived body composition for every non-series content slide when the selected template offers a compatible option.
- Do not repeat an exact body layout on adjacent non-series slides. Outside intentional series, avoid using the same exact source layout more than twice across the deck.
- Allow repeated layouts for genuinely similar content, step-throughs, controlled comparisons, recurring case pages, or data updates when repetition establishes a useful visual grammar.
- Keep one coherent whole-template system across the deck. Do not switch to unrelated whole templates page by page merely to manufacture variety.
- Use cards only when boundaries communicate grouping, equivalence, hierarchy, or state. Otherwise prefer spacing, alignment, scale, and a restrained divider.
- Replace long unstructured lists with a real information form such as grouping, timeline, matrix, process, comparison, or appendix.
- Let complex evidence pages be denser than section or synthesis pages; do not force every slide to have the same density.

## Migration and redesign protocol

When an uploaded deck is classified as content-only, do not use its visual system as the baseline. Extract its approved content, then set the visual baseline from the selected HiPPT managed template. Inspect the source deck's visuals only to detect accidental carryover.

Before adapting the selected HiPPT template, or when an explicit source-template exception exists, audit:

- masters, layouts, grid, margins, and recurring chrome;
- logo, palette, typography, radii, icons, charts, and image treatment;
- signature pages and recognizable brand cues to preserve;
- small type, weak hierarchy, filler decoration, inconsistent assets, and repeated generic layouts to retire;
- source content, notes, citations, and editable elements that must remain intact.

Modernize in this order and stop when the brief is satisfied:

1. typography and hierarchy;
2. spacing and alignment rhythm;
3. palette and contrast cleanup;
4. key-slide recomposition and layout rhythm;
5. image, chart, icon, and diagram quality;
6. transitions or animation only when requested and useful.

## Accessibility and release review

- Keep essential meaning available without color alone.
- When contrast can be measured, target at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text and essential graphical elements.
- Add a solid text panel, scrim, or crop change when text over imagery is unstable.
- Use real assets and real data whenever the slide implies evidence; label concepts and examples visibly.
- Inspect the full-deck montage for style drift, repetitive silhouettes, abrupt density changes, random accents, and decoration without a communication job.
- Inspect representative slides at full size for alignment, hierarchy, contrast, crop, and projection readability.

These principles adapt the contextual anti-slop and redesign discipline from the public Taste Skill to editable presentations: <https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill>. Web-only code, responsive layout, dark-mode, animation, and performance rules do not transfer automatically.
